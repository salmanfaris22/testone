      Mission

        From vision to operation

        What we stand for

        To advance sustainable aquaculture by proving it can work in harmony with the Gulf’s ecosystems and communities.

        We exist to make aquaculture work: for our ecosystems, for food security, and for future generations. In a region where seafood demand is growing and marine ecosystems are under strain, we believe in a better way: simpler, smarter, and grounded in nature.

        Our principles

        1. Grow responsibly, not rapidly
        2. Invest only what we need to learn
        3. Focus on animal welfare and ecosystem integrity
        4. Build trust through transparency
        5. Share knowledge and invite collaboration
        Our vision

        To nurture a sustainable future by cultivating what thrives naturally, with humility, care, and scientific rigour.

        This isn’t just a job. It’s a responsibility to the Gulf.

        Our regional role

        Our role is to serve the sea and the people who depend on it.

        • 50%lessWild Gulf fish stocks since 1990

          We supply seedstock from land-based hatcheries, easing pressure on wild populations.

        • 95%Of water recycled in our hatchery system

          Lower environmental footprint and energy use, aligned with national sustainability goals.

        A region hungry for change

        Demand for seafood in the UAE is growing at 8% annually, expected to reach 900,000 tonnes by 2030. Yet our local fisheries are in decline, and most aquaculture projects face challenges tied to over-engineered systems and species not suited to Gulf waters.

        Despite significant investment, aquaculture in the UAE has yet to reach its potential. At Gulf Aquaculture, we saw this not as a failure, but an opportunity to rethink, simplify, and rebuild from first principles.

        We’re doing things differently

        We believe success starts small. Our method is rooted in four pillars:

        1. Prioritise native species like grouper and red snapper
        2. Keep upland infrastructure minimal: just hatchery and nursery
        3. Move juveniles to sea cages early, reducing energy use
        4. Grow gradually through proof of concept, validation, and iteration

        We call this Local Species in Local Waters.

        Leadership interview

        A thoughtful approach to growth

        A conversation with Mohamed Razali Mohamed, Founder and General Manager of Gulf Aquaculture

        What inspired you to launch Gulf Aquaculture?
        I’ve always believed that food security starts in our own waters. But the models we saw here weren’t working: they were imported, high-cost, and environmentally mismatched. We saw a gap for a simpler, region-specific approach. That’s how Gulf Aquaculture began.
        What makes your approach different?
        We don’t chase complexity. We focus on native species, grow them naturally, and keep our systems lean. The difference is, we’re not trying to replicate European aquaculture. We’re building something Gulf-born and Gulf-suited.
        What’s your vision for the next 5 years?
        Scale. But responsibly. We want to prove this model can work nationally, and then regionally. And we want to be a partner to government and research institutions: not just a supplier, but a strategic contributor to the UAE’s food systems.

        “Every phase is tested before it’s scaled. That’s how we build trust, with nature and with investors.”

        What does success look like for Gulf Aquaculture?
        For us, success isn’t measured by how fast we grow, but by how well we grow. If we can reliably produce fry of local species to support the region’s aquaculture industry, raise healthy fish, protect the marine environment, and earn the trust of partners and regulators, that’s a strong foundation. Over time, we hope our model can inspire others to approach aquaculture in a more local and sustainable way.
        How do you approach uncertainty in a developing sector like this?
        Carefully. We know this industry is still taking shape in the region, so staying grounded is key. We start small, validate every step, and let production data and environmental conditions guide our decisions. It’s about being proactive without being reckless, and keeping long-term resilience at the center of what we do.

        “We’re not building a fish farm. We’re building trust, in the idea that the UAE can feed itself, responsibly.”

        Mohamed Razali Mohamed · Founder and General Manager, Gulf Aquaculture
